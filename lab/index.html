A FEW THINGS TO NOTICE

Every flat map makes a trade.

You can flatten the paper. You can’t flatten the globe without changing something.

01 / CONTACT

Start where they touch.

Along the equator, the globe and cylinder share a line. Scale is true there. Move away from it and the shadows stretch.

02 / DISTORTION

Watch the shadows grow.

Near the poles, rays travel almost parallel to the cylinder. Their shadows shoot toward infinity. Our finite paper stops at about 63° north and south.

03 / THE MODEL

A useful starting point.

This is a literal light-at-center experiment. Many maps, including Mercator and Lambert conformal conic, use mathematical rules beyond this shadow model.

The geometry behind the experiment +

The Earth is a unit sphere. Every shadow is computed by intersecting a ray from its center with the paper; the paper then unfolds without stretching. The cut edges are seams. Rays that miss the finite sheet are omitted. The gold rays are selected examples; the entire grid and coastline are projected.

Equal-circle overlays have the same 6° angular radius on the globe. They illustrate distortion, rather than infinitesimal Tissot indicatrices. A projection is one part of a coordinate reference system; this lab does not perform datum or EPSG transformations.
